THE   CIRCULAR   TEMPLE

No one saw him disembark in the unanimous night, no one saw the bamboo canoe sink into the sacred mud, but in a few days there was no one who did not know that the taciturn man came from the South and that his home had been one of those numberless villages upstream in the deeply cleft side of the mountain.

This circle was a temple devoured by ancient fires, profaned by the miasmal jungle. His immediate obligation was to dream.

The purpose which guided him was supernatural. He wanted to dream a man — to dream him in minute entirety — and impose him on reality.

At first his dreams were chaotic; then dialectic. Clouds of taciturn students filled a circular amphitheater. He was seeking a soul worthy of participating in the universe.

After countless nights, he dreamed an entire man — a young man who did not sit up or speak, who was unable to open his eyes. Night after night, the wizard dreamed him asleep.

Fire revealed itself — not tiger nor colt nor bull nor storm — but all of them at once. Fire promised animation.

The wizard instructed him in the rites of the universe. Secretly he feared separation. He destroyed all memory of apprenticeship.

Gradually he understood: his son was ready to be born. He sent him downstream.

With relief, with humiliation, with terror, he understood that he also was an illusion, that someone else was dreaming him.
